Can a Locksmith Make a Key Without the Original? Yes: Here Is How

Lost the only key? A locksmith does not need something to copy. There are three ways to originate a key, and we pick the fastest for your lock.

1. Cut by code

Many locks, padlocks, cabinets, cars and motorcycles have a key code stamped on the cylinder, the key tag or the owner's paperwork. We look up the bitting and cut a factory-fresh key on the code machine. Fastest and most accurate.

2. Decode the lock

We remove the cylinder or read the pins in place with a decoder, write down the depths, and cut the key to them. The lock is not damaged and your old keys, if any turn up, still work.

3. Impressioning

A blank is worked in the lock so the pins leave marks, then filed down until it turns. Used for locks that cannot be taken apart on site. Takes longer but needs no code and no disassembly.

Expect to show proof of ownership (ID with the address, lease, registration for a vehicle). If someone else may still have the lost key, rekeying for $15-$30 per lock is safer than just making a new key; details on the lost key replacement page.

Worn Keys, "Do Not Duplicate" Keys and Restricted Keys

Why copies of old keys stick

A duplicator traces the key you hand it. If that key is 15 years old, the copy inherits every rounded peak and every worn valley, and the copy of the copy is worse. We decode the key and cut to factory depths instead, so the new key often works better than the one you brought.

"Do Not Duplicate" is a request

The stamp has no legal force in the US, which the Associated Locksmiths of America has confirmed, and kiosks and stores refuse it mainly to avoid arguments. We copy a stamped key for the owner or the building manager with ID and authorization. Want real control over copies? Move to a restricted keyway.

Restricted keyways are the real thing

Medeco, Mul-T-Lock, Primus and Abloy blanks are sold only to authorized dealers and cut only for the registered owner. A tenant, an ex or a contractor cannot run off copies at a kiosk. We install these systems and cut the keys for them with your authorization card.

How a Key Is Cut Properly

1

Identify the blank

Keyway, length and head shape are matched to the right blank. A near-miss blank is why some copies go in but will not turn.

2

Duplicate or decode

A good original is traced on the duplicator. A worn original, or no original, is decoded and cut by code.

3

Deburr

The cut edges are brushed smooth so the key does not shave the pins or hang up in the keyway.

4

Test in your lock

Every key is tried in the lock it was made for before you pay. For car keys that includes starting the engine and testing the remote buttons.

Key Copy and Key Cutting FAQ: Staten Island

What people ask before they call about keys.

Where can I get a key copied near me in Staten Island?

You have three options: a self-service kiosk (minuteKEY, KeyMe) in some supermarkets and pharmacies, a hardware store counter, or a locksmith. Kiosks and hardware stores handle plain house keys only. A locksmith cuts every kind of key, including worn keys, car keys with chips, restricted keys and keys with no original. We come to you anywhere in Staten Island and cut most keys in under five minutes.

How much does it cost to get a key cut?

In Staten Island a standard house key copy is $5-$10, a padlock, mailbox or cabinet key $5-$12, a restricted or high-security key $25-$75 with authorization, a basic car key with no chip $35-$85, and a transponder car key $125-$195 cut and programmed. Keys cut during another service visit have no trip charge; a key-only visit has a small trip charge we quote on the phone.

Can you make a key without the original?

Yes. If you have no key at all we make one from the lock itself by reading the pins, impressioning a blank, or cutting to the manufacturer's code stamped on the lock or listed in the owner's manual. This is called key origination and costs $25-$75 for most house locks. For cars we cut by VIN or code and program the chip at the car. See our lost key replacement page for details.

Can you copy a worn key?

A worn key copied on a duplicator produces a copy with the same wear, which is why hardware-store copies of old keys often stick or fail. We decode the key or the lock and cut the new key to factory depths, so the copy works better than the original.

Can you copy a 'Do Not Duplicate' key?

The stamp is a request, not a law; the Associated Locksmiths of America confirms it carries no legal force in the US. We copy stamped keys for the owner or the building manager with ID and authorization. Keys on a patented restricted keyway (Medeco, Mul-T-Lock, Schlage Primus, Abloy) are different: the blanks are controlled, and we cut them only with the authorization card or a letter from the system owner.

Can a locksmith copy car keys, or do I need the dealer?

A locksmith copies almost every car key, including chip keys and remote fobs, and programs it at your car for 30-50% less than the dealer. The rare exceptions are a few brand-new encrypted models still limited to dealers. See car key replacement for prices by key type.

Can you copy a mailbox key?

For a private mailbox, a mailbox at a two-family house, or a mailroom box owned by a landlord, yes: we duplicate the key or make one from the lock. For USPS cluster boxes (the blue or silver multi-unit boxes), the post office owns the lock and only USPS can replace the key; we can help with a private replacement lock if the building allows it.

Can you copy an apartment building fob or access card?

Many RFID fobs and cards used on Staten Island building doors can be cloned in minutes; encrypted fobs cannot. Bring the fob and we tell you on the spot. If the building issues fobs itself, check your lease; some managements charge for lost fobs but prohibit copies.

How long does it take to cut a key?

A standard house key takes two to three minutes. A high-security or dimple key takes five to ten. A key made from the lock with no original takes 15-30 minutes. A car key with a chip takes 20-40 minutes including programming.

Do you cut keys for safes, padlocks and cabinets?

Padlocks, cabinets, desks, toolboxes, tubular locks on vending or display cases and most small safes, yes. We do not duplicate bank safe-deposit keys.

How many spare keys should I have, and where should I keep them?

One per adult in the household plus one spare with a neighbor or in a coded lockbox. Property managers usually keep two per unit. Order spares in one visit; the second and later copies of the same key are cheaper because the setup is done.

Do you make keys for landlords and businesses in bulk?

Yes. Property managers, co-op boards and contractors get per-key volume pricing, keys tagged by unit, and keys cut by code so every unit's key is a clean factory cut. See our Commercial Accounts page for Net-30 terms.
