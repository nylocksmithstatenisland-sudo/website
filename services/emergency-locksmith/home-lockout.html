Home Lockout Service in Staten Island, NY

Being locked out of your house or apartment is the most common emergency call we get in Staten Island, and it happens to careful people: the door swings shut while you take out the trash, a key snaps in a cold deadbolt, a smart lock battery dies, or the keys are simply gone. Our emergency locksmith team is mobile, licensed by the NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, insured, and based at 1775 Richmond Ave, so a technician is never far from any neighborhood on the island.

We open doors the way a locksmith should: through the lock, with picks, decoders and bypass tools, so your lock keeps working and your key still fits when we leave. Drilling is a last resort reserved for locks that have already failed, and we tell you before we do it. Once you are inside we can cut a new key, rekey the lock if the keys were lost, or fit a keypad so this does not happen again. If you have no key left at all, see lost key replacement.

Locked Out of Your Apartment? What Is Different in a Building

Apartment lockouts in Staten Island come with a few extra rules, and knowing them can save you the cost of the call.

Try the super first

NYC's Housing Maintenance Code requires buildings with nine or more apartments to have a superintendent or janitor who can be reached 24 hours a day. Many supers hold a key or can let you in for nothing. Smaller buildings and two-family houses usually have no super, and the landlord may be hours away.

You do not need landlord permission

You need to prove you live there, not get approval. A lease, an ID with the address, or the super vouching for you is enough. We are happy to call management with you if the building requires it, and we leave the lock exactly as we found it.

Apartment doors are harder than house doors

Most Staten Island apartment doors are steel with a deadbolt plus a jimmy-proof or mortise lock, so the credit-card trick fails every time. These are opened through the cylinder, which is why you want a locksmith with the right tools rather than a handyman with a crowbar.

Co-ops, condos and NYCHA

We work in co-ops, condos, rentals and NYCHA developments across the island. If your building has a front-door intercom or a doorman, let them know a locksmith is coming. Under NY Multiple Dwelling Law 51-c you may add your own extra lock to the apartment door, as long as the landlord gets a key on request.

Locked out by your landlord? That is different. A landlord, super or their worker who changes the lock or removes your door to keep you out without a court order is breaking the law. Call 911 or the NYC Tenant Helpline at 311; a locksmith cannot resolve that for you.

Picking and decoding

Pin-tumbler locks are opened by setting each pin to the shear line with picks or a decoder. The lock is unchanged and your key works afterward. This is the method for about nine out of ten lockouts.

Bypass tools

Some latches and lever locks can be opened by reaching the mechanism directly, which is faster than picking. It is the professional version of the credit-card trick, with tools that do not bend or damage the latch.

Key extraction

A key broken in the lock is pulled out with extractor hooks, then we pick the lock open. If the key broke because the cylinder is worn, we replace the cylinder so it does not happen again.

Drilling, last resort only

We drill only when a lock has already failed internally or a high-security cylinder cannot be decoded in reasonable time. We say so before we start and quote the replacement lock. A locksmith who reaches for the drill first is costing you a new lock.

A locksmith who will open any door for anyone is a locksmith who will open yours for a stranger. We verify that you live at the address, and we do it in a way that works when your wallet is locked inside.

Will the Police Open My Door? Does the Credit-Card Trick Work?

Police and fire department

The NYPD does not open doors for routine lockouts in Staten Island; officers will tell you to call a locksmith. The exception is an emergency: a toddler or an elderly relative alone inside, someone in medical distress, a stove or candle left burning. Then call 911. The FDNY will force the door, and the door, frame or lock will usually need repair afterward, which we can also handle.

Credit card, bobby pins and bump keys

A flexible card can slip a spring latch on an inward-swinging door with no deadbolt thrown. It does nothing on a deadbolt, a jimmy-proof lock or a mortise lock, which is what most Staten Island exterior doors have, and it often bends the latch plate. Bobby-pin picking and bump keys damage pins and springs; the lock then needs replacing. If the door has a deadbolt, skip the DIY and keep the lock.

While you wait

  • Stay somewhere lit and warm: a neighbor's porch, your car, a nearby store. On winter nights tell us if you have nowhere to wait and we will prioritize the call.
  • Gather whatever proof of residence you can reach, and have a payment method ready; we take cards and cash.
  • If a pet or a pot on the stove is inside, tell us when you call so we can plan to be quick.
  • Do not hire a second locksmith "just in case"; you will owe two trip fees.

Common Staten Island Lockouts and How Each Is Fixed

Keys locked inside

The door closed behind you. We open it, nothing else changes, and we can cut a spare before we leave so it does not happen twice. $75-$125 by day.

Lost or stolen keys

We open the door, then rekey every lock the key fit for $15-$30 each so whoever has the key cannot use it, and cut new keys on site. Stolen with your ID or a bag with your address? Rekey the same day.

Key broke in the lock

Common on cold mornings with old brass keys. We extract the piece, open the door and cut a new key from the halves. See emergency lock repair if the cylinder itself is worn.

Key turns but the door will not open

Usually a broken tailpiece, a failed latch, or a deadbolt that no longer retracts. We open the door through the mechanism and then repair or replace the lock.

Frozen or jammed deadbolt in winter

Moisture freezes inside the cylinder on exposed doors in Midland Beach, South Beach and along the shore. Forcing the key snaps it. We thaw and lubricate the lock and open it; a weather cover prevents the repeat.

Smart lock dead or code forgotten

We open through the backup key cylinder or power the keypad from a 9-volt battery or USB-C, then replace the batteries and reset the code. Ask about a smart lock with a key override if yours has none.

Locked out of the garage, basement or back door

Same service, same price. Garage side doors and basement doors are often where the only working key is kept, and they are usually easier to open than the front.

Locked out of a bedroom or bathroom

Privacy locks pop with a small tool through the hole in the knob. If a child has locked themselves in, call us and stay on the line; we will talk you through it while we drive.

How to Never Be Locked Out Again

  • Leave a spare with someone nearby. A neighbor two doors down beats a relative in New Jersey. We can cut a spare on the same visit.
  • Use a coded lockbox, not a fake rock. A wall-mounted lockbox with a four-digit code in a spot not visible from the street is what landlords and home-care agencies use for a reason.
  • Add a keypad deadbolt. A keypad or smart lock means no key to forget, and each family member gets a code. Installed for $100-$200 plus the lock; see smart lock installation.
  • Rekey when you move in. Previous owners, tenants, contractors and their friends may still hold keys. Rekeying is cheaper than a lockout.
  • Replace a sticking lock before it fails. If the key needs jiggling, the lock is telling you something. A worn cylinder is a $75-$150 repair; a lockout at 11 PM plus the repair is more.

How to Avoid Locksmith Scams When You Are Locked Out

Lockout calls attract bait-and-switch operators because you are stressed and in a hurry. Five checks, each taking a few seconds, filter them out.

Watch for

  • "$19 service" or "$29 lockout" ads; the real bill arrives at the door
  • A dispatcher who answers with a generic "locksmith service" instead of a business name
  • A technician in an unmarked car who insists the lock must be drilled
  • Cash only, or a price that doubles once the door is open
  • No NYC DCWP license, or refusing to give the number

What you get from us

  • The full price on the phone, before we dispatch, and the same price at the door
  • NYC DCWP locksmith license number on request, verifiable on the city's license lookup
  • Insurance through Ergo Next; certificate available for co-op and condo boards
  • Non-destructive entry first; drilling only with your approval
  • Cards and cash accepted, with an itemized receipt

Home Lockout FAQ: Staten Island

Straight answers to the questions people ask us from the doorstep.

I'm locked out of my house in Staten Island. What should I do first?

Check every other door and reachable window first, including the basement and side doors, which are often left on the knob latch only. Then call anyone who holds a spare key: a family member, neighbor, roommate, landlord or super. If none of that works, call us at (718) 831-6269. We confirm the price on the phone, arrive in about 20-30 minutes, and open most doors in 5-15 minutes without damage.

Who do I call when I'm locked out of my house?

A licensed locksmith is the right call for a routine lockout. Staten Island police do not open doors unless someone inside is in danger (a small child or elderly person alone, a stove left on, a medical issue); in that case call 911. Your landlord or super may hold a key if you rent. For everything else, call a locksmith with a NYC DCWP license, which we provide on request.

How much does it cost to unlock a house door in Staten Island?

A standard house or apartment lockout is $75-$125 during our daytime hours and $95-$150 on nights, weekends and holidays, including the trip and non-destructive entry. High-security locks such as Medeco or Mul-T-Lock run $125-$200 because they take longer to open. If a lock must be drilled, which is rare, we tell you before we start and quote the replacement lock. There is no separate service-call fee.

How long does it take a locksmith to get to my house and open the door?

We usually arrive within 20-30 minutes anywhere in Staten Island, from St. George to Tottenville. Once on site, a knob lock or standard deadbolt typically opens in 5-15 minutes. Mortise locks, jimmy-proof locks and high-security cylinders can take 20-40 minutes.

Will a locksmith open my door without ID?

We have to confirm you live there, but we know your wallet is often inside. We accept a driver's license or state ID with the address, a lease, a utility bill on your phone, or a neighbor, landlord or super who can vouch for you. If your ID is inside, we open the door and check it before we leave. We will not open a door for someone who cannot show they have a right to be there.

I'm locked out of my apartment. Can a locksmith help, or do I need my landlord?

You do not need your landlord's permission to have your own apartment door opened; you need to prove you live there. In buildings with nine or more apartments, NYC rules require a super or janitor to be reachable 24 hours a day, so try them first because it may be free. If the super is unreachable or the building has no super, call us. We work in co-ops, condos, rentals and NYCHA buildings across Staten Island.

Can a locksmith unlock a deadbolt?

Yes. Single-cylinder and double-cylinder deadbolts are opened by picking or decoding the cylinder, which leaves the lock working and your key unchanged. The credit-card trick only works on spring latches, so do not try it on a deadbolt; it will not open and it can bend the latch plate.

Can you open a jimmy-proof or mortise lock on an older Staten Island house?

Yes. Segal-style jimmy-proof rim locks and mortise locks are common on pre-war homes in St. George, Stapleton and Port Richmond and on many apartment doors. They are opened through the cylinder, not by forcing the door, so the lock stays usable afterward.

Can you open a smart lock or keypad lock if the battery died or I forgot the code?

Yes. Most smart locks have a hidden mechanical key cylinder we can open, and many accept emergency power from a 9-volt battery or USB-C so you can enter your code. After entry we can replace the batteries, reset the code or, on a retrofit lock, release the thumb-turn so you are not locked out again.

Is there a 24-hour locksmith for home lockouts in Staten Island?

We are available 24 hours a day Friday, Saturday and Sunday, and from 9 AM to 6 PM Monday through Thursday. We do not run overnight service on weeknights, and we say so up front rather than advertising 24/7 and not answering.

Will the police or fire department unlock my house for free?

Only in an emergency. If a child or elderly relative is alone inside, someone needs medical help, or something is burning, call 911; the fire department will force entry, and the door or frame will usually be damaged. For an ordinary lockout, police in Staten Island will tell you to call a locksmith.

Should I rekey or change my locks after a lockout?

If you simply left the keys inside, no. If the keys were lost or stolen, especially with anything that shows your address, rekey the lock the same visit so the old key no longer works. Rekeying is $15-$30 per lock and takes a few minutes; a new key is cut on the spot.

My key broke off in the lock. Can you get it out and still open the door?

Yes. We extract the broken piece with key-extractor tools, open the door, and cut a replacement key from the two halves or from the lock itself. The cylinder usually survives; if the key broke because the lock is worn, we can replace the cylinder on the same visit.

How do I avoid a locksmith scam when I'm locked out in Staten Island?

Be careful with ads that quote $19 or $29 service; the price usually jumps to $200+ at the door after the technician insists on drilling. Ask for the company's real name and NYC DCWP license number, get the total price on the phone, and expect a technician who arrives with picks, not just a drill. We quote the full price before we dispatch and it does not change at the door.
