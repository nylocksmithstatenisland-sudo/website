How Much Does a Replacement Car Key Cost? The Short Answer

A replacement car key in Staten Island costs between $35 and $450 from a mobile locksmith, depending almost entirely on the technology inside the key. A plain metal key for a pre-1998 car is $35-$85. A transponder key with an immobilizer chip, which is most cars from 1998 to 2015, is $125-$195. A remote-head key or separate fob is $150-$275. A smart key for a push-to-start car is $250-$450. Those prices include cutting, programming and the trip to your car.

Three things move the price inside those ranges: whether you still have a working key (a spare is cheaper than an all-keys-lost job), the make (European luxury brands cost more and sometimes need the dealer), and when you need it (weekend nights carry an after-hours rate). Everything below is what I quote and charge on real jobs across Staten Island, from St. George to Tottenville, not a national average.

If you start the car with a button and the key stays in your pocket, it is a smart key.

Spare Key vs All Keys Lost: Why the Price Changes

If you have a working key, the job is a duplicate: I read the chip, cut a matching blade, add the new key to the car's memory, and you have two keys. If you have no key at all, three things change.

  1. The blade has to come from somewhere. I either pull the key code from the VIN through a licensed database or decode your door lock by hand. Either adds 15-30 minutes.
  2. The immobilizer has to be told to trust a stranger. Adding a key when one exists is a two-minute handshake. Adding a key when none exists means putting the car in programming mode with a security code or, on some makes, reading the immobilizer module directly. That is the $75-$150 difference.
  3. The old keys should be erased. If the keys were stolen rather than lost, I delete every previous key from the car's memory so the stolen one cannot start it. That is included in an all-keys-lost job and is the main reason not to wait.

Practical advice: as soon as you have one working key, make a spare. A spare smart key costs $250-$450; the same key with nothing to copy from costs $325-$500 plus the stress of being stranded. Most people I meet on an all-keys-lost call had the car for years with a single key. If you are in that situation right now, the emergency car key replacement page explains exactly what happens on the call.

What to do if you lost your only car key

  • Check the obvious places once: pockets, bags, the car's door pocket, the ground around where you parked. Keys locked inside a running car are a car lockout, a cheaper job.
  • Find your registration or title and a photo ID. No locksmith or dealer will make a key without them.
  • Note the VIN from the base of the windshield or the registration, and whether the car is push-button start.
  • Call a mobile locksmith before a tow truck. A tow to the dealer is $75-$200 you do not need to spend.
  • If the keys may have been stolen with anything showing your address, ask for the old keys to be erased and consider rekeying the house too.

Hidden Costs Dealers and Discount Ads Leave Out

  • Towing. The dealer cannot come to you. With no working key, that is $75-$200 before the key is even discussed.
  • Diagnostic fees. Some dealers charge $100-$150 to "diagnose" an all-keys-lost car before programming.
  • Two-key minimums. Several Ford, GM and Chrysler models need two keys to complete programming. A locksmith brings a second blank; a dealer may sell you two keys.
  • Module replacement on older European cars. On some 2000s BMW, Mercedes and VW models, all-keys-lost means replacing or reflashing the immobilizer module, $400-$900. I tell you this on the phone, not at the car.
  • The $19 ad. A "$19 car key" or "$29 service call" ad is the start of a bill, not the end. Ask for the total price for your year, make and model before anyone is dispatched. If they will not give one, hang up.
  • After-hours rates. Weekend nights add $30-$60 with us; some operators double the price after 8 PM. Ask.

Does Insurance, AAA or a Credit Card Cover Car Key Replacement?

Usually not, with a few useful exceptions.

  • Standard auto insurance: no. Lost keys are not a covered loss.
  • Comprehensive coverage: only if the keys were stolen, and only after your deductible, which is typically $500 and therefore more than most keys cost. Filing a claim for a $300 key makes no sense.
  • Roadside assistance riders (GEICO, Progressive, Allstate, State Farm add-ons): pay a lockout, and sometimes $50-$100 toward a locksmith. They do not pay for fob programming.
  • AAA: the locksmith benefit is capped by membership level, roughly $50 on Classic, $100 on Plus and $150 on Premier, and AAA does not commit to replacing or programming fobs. Useful for a basic key, a small dent in a smart key.
  • Credit cards: some premium travel cards reimburse key replacement under car-rental or travel-inconvenience benefits. Check the guide to benefits.
  • Key replacement plans sold with new cars: worth using if you have one; the dealer will want to do the work.

How to Pay Less for a Car Key: 7 Things That Actually Work

  1. Make a spare while you still have one. It is the single biggest saving in this guide: $75-$150 plus a possible tow.
  2. Call a locksmith before a tow truck. We come to the car.
  3. Replace the battery first. Most "dead fobs" are dead batteries.
  4. Ask for an exact price, not a range. With year, make, model, key type and VIN, an honest locksmith gives one number.
  5. Get two keys at once. The second key in the same visit costs $60-$120 less because the car is already in programming mode.
  6. Supply your own fob only with the exact part number, new, and accept that there is no warranty.
  7. Schedule rather than panic. A weekday afternoon appointment avoids the after-hours rate.

Car Key Replacement Cost FAQ

How much does it cost to replace a car key in 2026?

In Staten Island a replacement car key costs $35-$85 for a basic metal key, $125-$195 for a transponder (chip) key, $150-$275 for a remote-head key or fob, and $250-$450 for a push-to-start smart key, cut and programmed by a mobile locksmith. Dealers charge roughly 40-60% more and usually need the car towed in. European luxury brands are quoted by VIN and can exceed these ranges.

How much does a lost car key cost to replace when I have no spare?

All-keys-lost jobs run about $75-$150 more than a spare because the locksmith must decode the lock or pull the key code and, on some models, reset the immobilizer. Expect $200-$300 for a transponder key and $325-$500 for a smart key with no working key. A dealer adds a tow of $75-$200 on top.

Is it cheaper to get a car key from a locksmith or the dealer?

A mobile locksmith is cheaper for almost every key: $125-$195 for a transponder key against $200-$350 at the dealer, and $250-$450 for a smart key against $350-$700. The locksmith also comes to the car, so there is no tow and no multi-day wait for a key ordered from the manufacturer. A few brand-new encrypted models are still dealer-only.

How much does a key fob replacement cost?

A key fob with remote buttons costs $150-$275 cut and programmed in Staten Island, and a smart proximity fob $250-$450. If the fob works but the buttons are weak, it is usually a $5-$10 battery, not a $250 fob. If you bought your own fob online, programming alone is $75-$125 when the part is correct and new.

How much does it cost to program a car key or fob?

Programming alone, when you supply a correct new key or fob, is $75-$125 in Staten Island. Used fobs from most manufacturers are locked to their original car and cannot be reprogrammed, which is why we recommend buying the fob through us or checking the part number before you order.

Can a locksmith make a car key without the original?

Yes. We cut the key by code from the VIN or by decoding the door lock, then program the chip at the car with a diagnostic tool. You will need the registration or title and photo ID. The job takes 30-60 minutes for most vehicles.

Does car insurance cover lost car keys?

Standard auto policies do not. Comprehensive may cover keys stolen with the car, subject to your deductible (usually $500, more than most keys cost). Roadside riders and AAA cap locksmith benefits at $50-$100 and rarely cover fob programming. Some credit cards reimburse key replacement as a travel benefit.

How much does a car key cost by make: Toyota, Honda, Ford, Nissan, BMW?

In Staten Island a transponder or remote key for Toyota, Honda, Ford, Chevrolet, Nissan, Hyundai or Kia is $125-$275 from a locksmith and $200-$400 at the dealer. Smart keys for those brands are $250-$450 locksmith versus $350-$600 dealer. BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Audi and Land Rover smart keys are quoted by VIN and run $350-$700 or more, with some models dealer-only.

How long does car key replacement take?

Cutting a basic key takes 5-10 minutes. A transponder key is 15-30 minutes including programming, a fob 20-40 minutes, and a smart key 30-60 minutes. We arrive in 20-30 minutes across Staten Island, so most people are driving again within about an hour of calling.

Why are smart keys so expensive?

A smart key is a small encrypted radio that the car's immobilizer must recognize. The part alone is $100-$250, programming needs a diagnostic tool and a security code from the manufacturer, and some makes require the car's module to be reset when all keys are lost. Labor and tooling, not the metal, drive the price.

Should I buy a cheap key fob on Amazon and have it programmed?

Sometimes. A new aftermarket fob with the exact FCC ID and part number can save $50-$100, and we program them for $75-$125. The risks are counterfeit chips that fail programming, used fobs that are locked to another car, and no warranty. Our keys carry a 90-day programming guarantee; a fob you supply does not.

What should I have ready when I call for a quote?

Year, make and model, whether it is push-button start, whether you have any working key, and the VIN if you can read it from the windshield or the registration. With those five facts we give an exact price, not a range, before we dispatch.
